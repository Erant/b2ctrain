# Replace registration, share the physics across clips

Only one of Gaussian Garments' four stages actually consumes synchronized multi-view video: **Stage 2 registration**, which tracks a fixed-topology garment mesh through every frame by photometric fitting against a random synchronized camera of that frame. Stage 1 needs one multi-view frame, and our static splat can stand in for it. Stage 3 needs registered meshes plus any one aligned image per frame. Stage 4, the ContourCraft fine-tune, never sees an image: it fits GNN weights, **per-vertex** Lamé/bending/mass fields and per-edge rest lengths to 3D vertex trajectories. The problem is therefore narrower than "Gaussian Garments needs multi-view video". We need a source of 3D garment motion, and a fitting target small enough that mutually inconsistent, physically unreliable Wan 2.2 clips can constrain it. My recommendation is **Strategy B**: simulate-and-compare, with one shared low-dimensional material vector (about 4 parameters) per garment, fitted jointly across clips, and per-clip nuisances (camera, body motion, initial state) that we largely know because we generate the clips ourselves. It should be anchored by a fabric-type prior and correspondence-free losses (Strategy C). **Strategy A**, which expands one generated clip into synchronized views with a human novel-view video model and then runs Gaussian Garments as-is, should run as a parallel experiment, not as the main path. A fixes geometric consistency, but it fixes neither physical fidelity nor the over-parameterization of Stage 4. Before any of this is trusted, one experiment matters more than the rest: measure whether Wan's cloth motion on a 4D-Dress/ActorsHQ subject matches the real capture. No published benchmark covers cloth fidelity in generated video. Primary PDFs (arXiv, CVF, project pages) could not be fetched during the research, so the Gaussian Garments facts below come from its **source code**. Paper-level numbers for most other methods rest on abstracts and search snippets; the last section lists what is unverified.

## Only registration needs synchronized views; the rest can be fed from our splat

The released code makes the dependency structure explicit. Stage 1 runs COLMAP with known poses on a **single template frame**: MVS → Poisson (depth 13) → cut with garment masks → pyacvd to **8,000 vertices** → a UV unwrap that "has to be done manually" in Blender ([initialisation_utils.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/utils/initialisation_utils.py); [README](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/README.md)). The authors' own ActorsHQ replacement for Stage 1 is already "splat → mesh": train a 3DGS, render stereo pairs, get depth, mask the garment with GroundingDINO+SAM, fuse with TSDF, ACVD-remesh ([ActorsHQ-for-GG](https://github.com/hlimach/ActorsHQ-for-Gaussian-Garments)). That is structurally the chain b2ctrain's meshification plan already specifies: `probe --depth` → `mesh-fuse` (TSDF with SAM body prior) → `mesh-refine` (Sapiens normals) → `mesh-unwrap` (xatlas) ([docs/mesh-plan.md](../docs/mesh-plan.md)). Two changes make it a garment template. First, the per-view garment masks become renders of only the Gaussians whose `seg_label` is a clothing class. Second, the result is post-processed into a single-layer open shell: drop faces inside or near the SAM-3D-Body proxy, keep openings at neck, cuffs and waist, and remesh to about 8k near-uniform triangles.

Stage 2 is the stage that needs synchronized views. For each frame, every iteration samples **a random camera from that frame's synchronized set**. The loss is masked L1×0.8 + (1−SSIM)×0.2, plus bending (λ=0.03), stretching (λ=0.01), SMPL-X body collision (λ=1e3, last 2k iterations) and a "virtual edge" term (λ=0.05) that stops the front and back layers from collapsing. The budget is 5k iterations per frame, warm-started, or "a few minutes" per frame ([s2_registration.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/s2_registration.py); [mesh_model.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/mesh_model.py)). With one camera per frame, only those energies constrain the unseen side, and the depth ambiguity that SAFT needed two dedicated regularizers to control comes back ([SAFT, ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Stotko_SAFT_Shape_and_Appearance_of_Fabrics_from_Template_via_Differentiable_ICCV_2025_paper.html)).

Stage 3 trains a StyleUNet that maps UV-space ambient occlusion (baked in Blender with the body), normals and view direction to per-Gaussian colour and offset residuals. It samples independent (sequence, frame, camera) tuples, so one image per frame is enough if mesh–image alignment is good ([avatar_net.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/avatar_net.py); [dataloader.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/dataloader.py)). Trained only on our static orbit, it would see a single geometry and extrapolate badly on shading. PGC's alternative is documented to work from one static pose: composite a **high-pass of the Gaussian render with a low-pass of the physically shaded mesh** ([PGC, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Guo_PGC_Physics-Based_Gaussian_Cloth_from_a_Single_Pose_CVPR_2025_paper.html)).

Stage 4 is image-free and heavily parameterized, and that matters most for everything that follows. The ContourCraft notebook converts registrations to `.pkl` mesh sequences and SMPL-X to AMASS-style `.npz`. It then fine-tunes the **GNN weights** (Adam lr 1e-7 from the step-45000 checkpoint), **per-vertex** Lamé μ (bounded 15,909–63,636), Lamé λ (3,535–93,334), bending (6.4e-8–1.3e-3) and mass (0.043–0.7), plus **per-edge rest-length multipliers**. The loss is L2 vertex distance (weight 1e13) plus boundary, dihedral-angle and Green-strain terms. Supervised rollouts alternate with self-supervised physics steps on AMASS CMU. The optimizer steps once per simulated frame with the previous state detached, so gradients are one step long ([base.yaml](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/configs/finetune/base.yaml); [pervertex.py](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/material/pervertex.py); [runners/finetune.py](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/runners/finetune.py)). **Friction and damping are not fitted.** The default split holds out the first sequence, so at least **two registered sequences** are required, and the fine-tune takes about **24 h** ([gaugar.py](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/utils/gaugar.py); [GaussianGarments.ipynb](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/GaussianGarments.ipynb)). The per-vertex material bounds also cap what any fit can express: a fabric stiffer or heavier than the pretrained range is out of reach without retraining.

| Stage | Consumes | Needs sync multi-view video? | What we feed it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Geometry + UV | one multi-view frame, garment masks, cameras | No (one frame) | splat → `mesh-fuse`/`mesh-refine` with Sapiens2 clothing-label masks → single-layer shell → ACVD 8k → xatlas UV |
| 1b Appearance init (`s2 -tf`) | template frame, all cameras | No | bind existing garment splats to faces/UVs, or refit a UV Gaussian texture on the orbit with the mesh frozen |
| 2 Registration | every frame × several synchronized views, SMPL-X per frame | **Yes, the only hard dependency** | Strategy A (synthesized views) or B (monocular tracking / simulate-and-compare) |
| 3 Appearance net | registered mesh + one image per sample | No, but needs diverse geometry | orbit images for albedo; PGC-style high/low-pass for dynamic shading |
| 4 Behaviour fit | 3D mesh sequences (≥2) + SMPL-X; no images | No, but needs 3D trajectories | restricted to a global material vector; per-vertex/GNN fine-tune only if Strategy A yields clean registrations |

Driving requires an SMPL-X body, both at fit time (collision) and at inference (ContourCraft rollouts from AMASS `.npz`) ([GaussianGarments.ipynb](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/GaussianGarments.ipynb)). Our SAM-3D-Body/MHR proxy therefore needs an SMPL-X fit. One internal data point from b2ctrain matters here: per-frame SAM-3D-Body fits on generated orbit frames "do not see the swings and add ~5 px of noise", while learning per-view joint rotations inside the trainer worked ([docs/STATUS.md](../docs/STATUS.md)). Per-frame monocular HMR on Wan clips should be expected to be too noisy as cloth-driving input. The body should come from the motion we condition Wan on, refined in-loop.

## Three routes differ in what they share across clips

The research turned up no peer-reviewed method that pools many independent monocular clips with *different* motions of the same garment into one simulator or material fit. PhysDreamer and Physics3D use generated video, but for objects under volumetric MPM ([PhysDreamer](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13026v2); [Physics3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04338v3)). MatPhys names the neighbouring failure mode, per-scene inverse optimization producing inconsistent parameters for the same material, and answers it with a shared material codebook ([MatPhys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19386)). Unsynchronized-video 4DGS methods such as SyncTrack4D estimate per-camera time offsets for **one** event seen by N cameras ([SyncTrack4D](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04315v1)). Our clips show different motions, so no time offset aligns them, and those methods do not apply. That leaves three designs.

### A: expand one generated clip into synchronized views, then run Gaussian Garments nearly as-is

The pipeline: render the splat from a known camera, then generate a "hero" clip with Wan2.2-Animate. Wan2.2-Animate is driven by skeletons rendered from our SMPL-X motion, is Apache 2.0, and runs about 80 GB for the 14B I2V models ([Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)). Next, expand the hero clip into N synchronized views with a human novel-view video model, then run Stages 2–4. The candidate models all have code. **Flex4DHuman** is built on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, conditions only on relative camera pose and text, and reports beating prior work on DNA-Rendering and ActorsHQ ([Flex4DHuman](https://github.com/Andy-Cheng/Flex4DHuman)). **4DAnyone** targets "reconstruction-grade" views for 4DGS ([4DAnyone](https://github.com/ant-research/4DAnyone)). **MV-Performer** uses depth warping plus normal conditioning and is trained on MVHumanNet; its stated failure mode is poor depth ([MV-Performer](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07190v1)). Motion diversity comes from several hero clips, each expanded independently, which is exactly the "several multi-view sequences" Stage 4 wants. Our splat is an unusually strong extra input: we can supply true multi-view reference renders and proxy depth/normals at t=0, which is what MV-Performer-style and GEN3C-style conditioning consume ([GEN3C](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/GEN3C)).

The known problems are specific. 4DAnyone's core finding is that camera-controlled video diffusion looks right per view but **loses consistency when scaled to the tens of views that 4DGS needs**, because views have to be split into groups beyond one DiT context ([4DAnyone](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20335)). Gaussian Garments registration wants roughly that many. Unseen-side fabric is hallucinated a second time by a model trained on everyday clothing. No novel-view paper evaluates loose-garment secondary motion. Most importantly, A delivers *consistent* geometry, not *physically correct* motion. Feeding those registrations to an unmodified Stage 4 fits thousands of per-vertex material values and GNN weights to whatever the generator invented. A's value is that it reuses the reference implementation almost unchanged, and it gives a direct comparison point for B.

### B: per-clip tracking of our template, with physics shared across clips

B is the Shape-from-Template recipe, with a simulator in the loop and extended to N clips. Physics-guided SfT recovers shape *and* stretch/shear/bending from monocular RGB by pixel-wise comparison through a neural surrogate, 400–500× faster than φ-SfT ([CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Stotko_Physics-guided_Shape-from-Template_Monocular_Video_Perception_through_Neural_Surrogate_Models_CVPR_2024_paper.html)). SAFT extends this to appearance with a **2.64×** lower 3D error, on fabric-only scenes, not clothed humans ([SAFT](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Stotko_SAFT_Shape_and_Appearance_of_Fabrics_from_Template_via_Differentiable_ICCV_2025_paper.html)). The capture-based physics avatars show how few parameters real video pins down. PhysAvatar fits density, membrane and bending stiffness by **finite differences** over **24 frames** ([PhysAvatar](https://github.com/y-zheng18/PhysAvatar)). MPMAvatar fits density, Young's modulus and a rest-shape scale over **25 frames, 200 iterations** ([MPMAvatar](https://github.com/KAISTChangmin/MPMAvatar)).

B comes in two variants. **B1, track-then-fit**, runs a monocular Stage 2 per clip and feeds Stage 4 with a visibility-weighted vertex loss. It is cheap to build, but it bakes the monocular depth errors into the targets. **B2, simulate-and-compare**, is the recommended one. For each candidate material vector θ, roll out ContourCraft from the clip's known SMPL-X motion. Render the garment Gaussians (bound to the simulated mesh) from the clip's camera. Score the render against the clip with silhouette/garment-mask IoU, Sapiens normals, and 2D tracks from CoTracker3 or lifted 3D tracks from SpatialTrackerV2/TAPIP3D ([SpatialTrackerV2](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Xiao_SpatialTrackerV2_Advancing_3D_Point_Tracking_with_Explicit_Camera_Motion_ICCV_2025_paper.html); [TAPIP3D](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117634)), with down-weighted RGB. Optimize θ jointly over all clips, with per-clip nuisances: camera refinement, small SMPL-X residuals, garment initial state, an appearance code, and optionally a time-warp. Because the fit has four or so global parameters, gradient-free search (finite differences/SPSA, as PhysAvatar, MPMAvatar and the VDS fork do) is viable. That avoids backpropagating through long ContourCraft rollouts, where the reference fine-tune itself uses only one-step gradients.

Our setting is better-posed than in-the-wild monocular capture because **we author the clips**. PhysDreamer's trick generalizes: generate each clip from a render of our splat at a known camera, so frame 0 is registered by construction ([PhysDreamer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-72627-9_22)). Drive Wan with skeletons rendered from a known SMPL-X sequence, so body motion is known up to Wan's adherence. A per-clip body-residual mechanism already exists in b2ctrain in spirit: per-view joint rotations learned from the torque of splat gradients ([docs/STATUS.md](../docs/STATUS.md)). What B does not fix is the physics of the source video. If Wan's skirts do not lag or overshoot like cloth, B2 faithfully fits the wrong material. The low-dimensional, simulator-constrained parameterization limits the damage, because it cannot represent non-physical motion, but it cannot detect it.

### C: correspondence-free and statistical supervision

C sidesteps alignment entirely and works in four ways. **Spectral and flow statistics** are the oldest version. Bouman et al. recover stiffness and area weight from fabric under *unknown* wind ([ICCV 2013](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/html/Bouman_Estimating_the_Material_2013_ICCV_paper.html)). Runia et al. use a spectral decomposition layer over the video volume's temporal power spectrum ([CVPR 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05065)). "Unphased Wrinkles" uses a wrinkle-spectrum loss that gives consistent elasticity estimates across different wrinkle patterns of the same fabric, i.e. it is phase-invariant ([arXiv 2212.08790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08790)). Masked-flow magnitude histograms and hem-displacement spectra per clip are cheap B2 add-ons that survive per-clip geometric drift. A **feed-forward material encoder trained on synthetic rollouts** follows Yang et al. (CNN+LSTM trained on simulation; [ICCV 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2017/html/Yang_Learning-Based_Cloth_Material_ICCV_2017_paper.html)) and the 2026 codebook methods MatPhys and EgoPhys ([EgoPhys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16202)). Sample θ within ContourCraft's bounds and motions from AMASS, simulate on our proxy, render through our splat with randomized cameras, and train video features → posterior over θ. Each Wan clip then becomes one noisy measurement, and the per-clip posteriors combine robustly. The research found no such encoder for garments on moving bodies, so this piece is new work. A **fabric-type prior** follows Image2Garment: a fine-tuned VLM predicts composition, family, areal density and thickness, and Random Forests trained on a small measurement set map these to bending/shear/stretch/buckling stiffness, damping and friction ([Image2Garment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09658)). Classic measured databases hold on the order of **ten** fabrics ([Wang et al. 2011](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7g35q2x7/qt7g35q2x7.pdf)). Mapping CLO-style parameters onto ContourCraft's Lamé/bending/density vector is an unsolved mapping step. **Score distillation from Wan 2.2** follows DreamPhysics ([DreamPhysics](https://github.com/tyhuang0428/DreamPhysics)) and OmniPhysGS ([OmniPhysGS](https://wgsxm.github.io/projects/omniphysgs/)). It needs no observed clip at all, but it scores plausibility, not a match, and all published instances are object-centric volumetric MPM.

The closest artifact to our idea is the unofficial **`SamipSGz/mpm-avatar-vds`** (July 2026). It replaces MPMAvatar's MSE geometric loss with SDS through a frozen **Wan 2.2 I2V 14B**, loading only the low-noise expert to save about 28 GB. It adds friction as a fourth parameter beside density, Young's modulus and rest scale, and optimizes by SPSA for 100 iterations on ActorsHQ a1 frames 460–469, with penetration, stretch and smoothness regularizers ([mpm-avatar-vds](https://github.com/SamipSGz/mpm-avatar-vds)). **It has no published results or validation.** Treat it as a code starting point for a C4 regularizer, not as evidence that SDS recovers cloth material.

| | A: synthesize sync views | B2: simulate-and-compare, shared θ | C: statistical / prior / SDS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared across clips | nothing (each hero clip becomes a full MV sequence) | material θ (~4 params), garment template, appearance | material posterior / prior |
| Gaussian Garments reuse | Stages 2–4 nearly unchanged | Stage 1/3 representation; Stage 4 simulator frozen, material only | ContourCraft as forward model |
| Known to work | human NVS on DNA-Rendering/ActorsHQ; GG on real MV | SfT/SAFT on fabric; 3-param fits from 24–25 MV frames | spectral fits on fabric-in-wind; VLM priors; SDS on objects |
| Open | cloth fidelity of NVS; view-count consistency; per-vertex overfit | pooling many incoherent clips (no prior art); loose-garment monocular accuracy | garment-on-body encoder; CLO→ContourCraft mapping; SDS fabric discrimination |
| Main risk | hallucinated geometry fit as if real | fits the generator's non-physics faithfully | weak signal; plausibility ≠ accuracy |
| Cost | NVS per clip + minutes/frame registration + ~24 h Stage 4 | N clips × rollouts × ~100–200 function evals | synthetic dataset + encoder training; SDS at 14B |

## A four-phase plan that ships animation before it learns physics

The ordering follows one principle: every phase produces animatable garments, and later phases only move θ away from a prior when validation shows that the video evidence improves held-out motion.

| Phase | Deliverable | Concrete components | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0: Asset conversion (weeks) | Sim-ready garment per subject, animated with prior material | clothing-label mask renders → `mesh-fuse`/`mesh-refine` → shell extraction against SAM-3D-Body → ACVD ~8k → xatlas UV; bind garment splats to faces (GG face-local frames); SMPL-X fit to the MHR proxy; pretrained ContourCraft + VLM fabric-type → θ₀; PGC-style high/low-pass shading | clean rollouts on AMASS without explosions or penetrations; visual review |
| 1: Validation harness | Reference fits and baselines on real MV data | GG on ActorsHQ via the adapter, plus the released 4D-Dress checkpoints (`00122_outer`, `00134_upper`, …) as upper bounds; single-view holdout splits; synthetic identifiability sweep | harness reproduces GG held-out error; sweep says which θ components are identifiable |
| 2: Strategy B2 + C1/C3 | Shared-θ fit from authored Wan clips | render-conditioned Wan2.2-Animate clips from known cameras and SMPL-X; mesh-bound Gaussian render with vertex gradients (new b2ctrain mode on `meshdepth.cu`/`deform.cu` infrastructure); silhouette, normal, track and spectral losses; per-clip nuisances; SPSA/FD over θ; robust (median/heavy-tailed) pooling with θ₀ as prior | beats θ₀ on held-out real motion in the harness; stable across clip subsets |
| 3: Strategy A (parallel) and C2/C4 | Comparison and amortized/prior upgrades | Flex4DHuman/4DAnyone expansion of hero clips → GG Stage 2 → Stage 4 material-only vs full; synthetic-rollout material encoder; optional Wan SDS regularizer from mpm-avatar-vds | adopt per-vertex/GNN fine-tune only if it beats B2 on held-out real motion |

Two engineering notes follow from the code. Gaussian Garments' Gaussian texture is a **512×512 UV raster**, one Gaussian per covered texel, bound with barycentrics and re-posed from per-face centre, orientation and scale ([mesh_gaussian_model.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/mesh_gaussian_model.py)). That is a small, well-specified binding to reimplement in b2ctrain's CUDA renderer. It is also the piece Strategy B needs anyway, to push image gradients onto vertex positions and to render simulated meshes. Stage 4, by contrast, lives in ContourCraft under MIT. Its rollout and material modules can be reused directly as B2's forward model with the GNN frozen and `pervertex.Material` collapsed to a global vector ([ContourCraft LICENSE](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/LICENSE)).

## Validation hinges on a generator-fidelity test nobody has published

The protocol has four layers, and the third decides whether the whole approach is worth continuing. **Layer 0 is synthetic identifiability, with no generator involved.** Sample θ inside ContourCraft's bounds, simulate on our proxy with AMASS motions, render single-camera clips through our garment Gaussians, and check whether B2 and the encoder recover θ. The quasi-static drape mostly constrains the bending-to-weight ratio. Dynamic cues (post-stop oscillation, flutter spectra) add stiffness/mass and damping information, and Bouman's two-parameter recovery under unknown forcing is consistent with that ([ICCV 2013](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/html/Bouman_Estimating_the_Material_2013_ICCV_paper.html)). The ratio argument itself comes from physics, not from any paper verified here. No sensitivity study of HOOD/ContourCraft parameters from monocular video was found, so Layer 0 fills that gap for us.

**Layer 1 is a real single-view holdout.** On ActorsHQ (160 synchronized 12 MP cameras at 25 fps ([HumanRF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06356))) and on 4D-Dress (per-vertex garment labels, SMPL-X, 520 sequences ([4D-Dress](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Wang_4D-DRESS_A_4D_Dataset_of_Real-World_Human_Clothing_With_Semantic_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf))), build "incoherent clip sets" that mimic our setting: *different sequences* of the same outfit, one random camera each. Fit B2 and A on those. Score four things against held-out sequences: per-vertex/Chamfer garment error against 4D-Dress scans, PSNR/LPIPS from held-out cameras, parameter agreement with the full multi-view Gaussian Garments fit, and error relative to the θ₀ prior baseline. **Layer 2 is generator fidelity.** Render frame 0 of a real ActorsHQ/4D-Dress sequence from a real camera, drive Wan2.2-Animate with that sequence's real SMPL-X, and compare generated and real footage on garment-mask IoU over time, hem lag, masked-flow spectra and wrinkle spectra. Then run B2 on generated versus real single-view clips of the same motions. The θ gap between the two is the generator-induced bias, measured directly. **Layer 3 covers our own subjects**, where there is no ground truth: K-fold over clips (fit on K−1, predict the held-out clip's silhouette dynamics), bootstrap variance of θ across clip subsets, and a clip-rejection audit for garment topology changes.

## Physics fidelity, identifiability and licensing are the binding risks

**Generated cloth physics is the largest and least-measured risk.** On VideoPhy-2 the best model, Wan2.1-14B, scores **21.9%** joint semantic-plus-physical adherence on the hard split ([VideoPhy-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06800)). Physics-IQ found physical understanding "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism", with the original best at **29.5/100** ([Physics-IQ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09038)). Neither benchmark has a cloth category, and no Wan 2.2 or Animate result was found. Skeleton-driven human animation is also known for identity drift, and implicit-motion variants "lose fine-grained dynamics" ([Wan-Animate-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06009)). The expected failure modes (texture swimming, hemline morphing, cloth sticking to the body, missing momentum) are inferences; no source quantifies them for garments. **Identifiability** is the second risk: absolute stiffness and mass trade off, per-scene fits drift between scenes ([MatPhys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19386)), and Gaussian Garments' per-vertex fields would overfit generator noise. Fit ratios and a global θ, and keep the prior. **Licensing** is the third. The Gaussian Garments repo has **no LICENSE file** (checked at commit ceb66fc) and should be treated as all-rights-reserved ([repo](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments)). ContourCraft is MIT, PhysAvatar is MIT except bundled Codim-IPC and its rasterizer ([PhysAvatar LICENSE](https://github.com/y-zheng18/PhysAvatar/blob/main/LICENSE.md)), and Wan 2.2 and Wan-Animate-2 are Apache 2.0 ([Wan-Animate-2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan-Animate-2)). SMPL-X, AMASS, ActorsHQ and 4D-Dress carry their own, typically non-commercial, terms; that is general knowledge and was not re-verified. The licences of MPMAvatar, Flex4DHuman, 4DAnyone and MV-Performer were not checked. Secondary risks: fixed topology (no opening jackets, and tucked or occluded garments break Stage 1); SMPL-X-only collision for loose garments; a bounded material range in the pretrained GNN; and Wan-family control models trained on about 81-frame clips at 16 fps ([ComfyUI Wiki, secondary](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/tutorial/advanced/video/wan2.2/wan2-2-fun-control)), which forces chunking for long motions.

**What could not be verified.** The Gaussian Garments paper PDF was never read, so paper-reported camera counts, resolution, frames and sequences per garment, quantitative results, ablations and stated limitations are unknown. The "15 garments, 13 from 4D-Dress" figure comes from a search summary. Whether it used raw 4D-Dress images or scan renders is also unknown; the notes conflict between "1k multi-view images" and "24 rendered views". Also resting on abstracts or snippets only: the SAFT 2.64× figure and its code status; the parameter sets in Runia and Unphased Wrinkles; the internals of PhysDreamer, DreamPhysics and OmniPhysGS; PGC's choice of simulation material; view counts, resolution and licences for Flex4DHuman and 4DAnyone; the granularity of Wan-Animate-2's viewpoint control; Wan-Animate's 30 fps output; Image2Garment's dataset and code release; SMAGA and MonoCloth details. Wan 3.0's openness is contested between secondary sources, although the official GitHub org lists no Wan 2.5+ repo. mpm-avatar-vds has no results at all.

## Conclusion

Gaussian Garments' real bottleneck is not multi-view video but 3D trajectories plus a behaviour fit with thousands of degrees of freedom. The first can be synthesized. The second should simply not be attempted from generated evidence. The problem therefore becomes a Bayesian update of a handful of material parameters, starting from a fabric-type prior and informed by clips whose camera, first frame and body motion we control. That formulation survives mutual inconsistency, because each clip is only one noisy measurement. It also leaves a clean upgrade path to the full per-vertex fine-tune if Strategy A's synthesized registrations prove trustworthy.

The pipeline is not the novel risk; generator fidelity is. The Layer 2 experiment, real SMPL-X driving Wan from a real frame-0 render and compared with real footage, costs little, answers whether any of this can beat a VLM-chosen default material, and would itself be a publishable result: pooling incoherent generated clips into garment physics has no prior art, and no one has measured Wan's cloth against ground truth.
