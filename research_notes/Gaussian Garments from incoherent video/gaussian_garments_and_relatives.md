## Q1. The full Gaussian Garments pipeline: inputs, geometry, registration, Gaussian texture, appearance model, behavior fitting

### Takeaway
Gaussian Garments has four stages. (1) A garment mesh is reconstructed from **one multi-view frame** (COLMAP MVS with known cameras → Poisson → cut out with garment masks → ACVD remesh to about 8k vertices), and then **UV-unwrapped by hand in Blender**. (2) This template is **tracked through every frame of every multi-view video** by per-frame photometric optimization of a "mesh + Gaussian texture" representation, with physics-like regularizers. (3) An albedo Gaussian texture plus a StyleUNet "shadow" network are trained. The network maps UV-space ambient-occlusion, normal and view-direction maps to per-Gaussian color and position offsets, so appearance depends on geometry, not on pose. (4) A pretrained **ContourCraft GNN is fine-tuned** on the registered mesh sequences. The fine-tune jointly optimizes the GNN weights, **per-vertex material** (Lamé μ, Lamé λ, bending coefficient, vertex mass/density) and **per-edge rest-length multipliers** (rest geometry). The loss is an L2 vertex loss against the registrations, and the fine-tune alternates with self-supervised physics steps on AMASS. It takes about 24 h.

**Required inputs (code-verified, DataPreparation.md)**
- Per subject (one garment) you need one or more **sequences**. Each sequence is a multi-view video with a folder per camera, holding `rgb_images/`, binary `garment_masks/` (garment of interest) and `foreground_masks/` (body plus all garments) per frame. You also need `cameras.json` (3×3 intrinsics and 3×4 extrinsics per camera) and a `smplx/` folder with a per-frame `.pkl` of SMPL-X parameters and a `.ply` body mesh. SMPL-X model files and the COINS SMPL-X vertex segmentation go in the aux folder. — [DataPreparation.md](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/DataPreparation.md)
- For ActorsHQ, garment masks are generated with GroundingDINO (text prompt such as "green_dress") and SAM. Only the portrait cameras are used, because GroundingDINO produced false positives and negatives in horizontal frames. — [ActorsHQ-for-GG README](https://github.com/hlimach/ActorsHQ-for-Gaussian-Garments)

**Stage 1: geometry initialization (code-verified)**
- `s1_initialisation.py` runs COLMAP feature extraction with garment masks, exhaustive matching, and `point_triangulator` **with the known camera poses** (from `txt/`). It then runs `image_undistorter`, `patch_match_stereo` and `stereo_fusion` on the **template frame only**. Post-processing does: background/outlier removal → Poisson reconstruction (depth 13) → keep the largest component → `parse_scan` (cut out the garment using the masks) → **pyacvd clustering to 8,000 vertices** → `template.obj`. — [initialisation_utils.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/utils/initialisation_utils.py)
- "For the template frame, use the one where the whole surface of the garment is fully visible and where the pose is similar to the first frame of each sequence." The UV unwrap "**have to be done manually**" in Blender and saved as `template_uv.obj`. — [GG README](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/README.md)
- The alternative Stage 1 for ActorsHQ works like this: sparse COLMAP with known extrinsics → **gs2mesh**, i.e. train a 3DGS, render stereo pairs, get stereo depth, mask the garment with GroundingDINO+SAM, fuse with TSDF → cleaned, ACVD-remeshed garment `template.obj`. The seams/UV must still be added by hand. Advice from that README: pick a frame with the hands away from the body, and pick garments that nothing occludes (shirts that are not tucked in, no hair over them). — [ActorsHQ-for-GG README](https://github.com/hlimach/ActorsHQ-for-Gaussian-Garments)
- The paper counts "appearance initialization" as part of Stage 1, but in the code it runs as `s2_registration.py -tf <frame>`, which optimizes the Gaussians plus the mesh on the single template frame (10,000 iterations plus 2,000 collision iterations). — [GG README](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/README.md); [s2_registration.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/s2_registration.py)

**Gaussian texture binding (code-verified)**
- Gaussians live in the template's **UV texture space**. A 512×512 UV raster (`texture_size=512`) is rasterized to a `bind_map` that stores the face id of each texel. Each covered texel becomes one Gaussian, bound to that face with 2D barycentric coordinates computed from the UV triangle. Each frame, the face center, face orientation matrix and face scale are recomputed from the deformed mesh (`update_face_coor`). Gaussian position, rotation and scale are expressed relative to the face frame, and scale is multiplied by the face scale. Gaussian attributes can be exported as UV maps (features_dc/rest, scaling, rotation, opacity). — [mesh_gaussian_model.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/mesh_gaussian_model.py); [avatar_gaussian_model.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/avatar_gaussian_model.py); [s3_appearance.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/s3_appearance.py)

**Stage 2: registration / tracking (code-verified)**
- For each frame t of a sequence, the loop picks **a random training camera from that frame's synchronized views** at every iteration, renders the mesh-bound Gaussians, and minimizes: masked L1 × (1−0.2) + (1−SSIM) × 0.2, **plus mesh energy terms**. The energy terms are discrete **bending** (λ=0.03) and **stretching** (λ=0.01) relative to the template, **body collision** against that frame's SMPL-X mesh (λ=1e3, active in the last 2,000 iterations), and a "virtual edge" term (λ=0.05). The virtual-edge term penalizes compression of edges between opposing faces (normal dot < −0.7), which stops the front and back layers from collapsing. — [s2_registration.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/s2_registration.py); [mesh_model.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/mesh_model.py); [arguments/__init__.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/arguments/__init__.py); [geometry_utils.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/utils/geometry_utils.py)
- Iteration budget: template frame 10k plus 2k collision iterations. First frame of another sequence 15k ("cross" scene, initialized from the template sequence, with a KD-tree nearest body face for collision). Each subsequent frame 5k, warm-started from the previous frame. The README says "Registering geometry to one frame takes a few minutes." Output: `stage2/<seq>/meshes/frame_XXXXX.obj`, one mesh per frame with fixed template topology. — [s2_registration.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/s2_registration.py); [GG README](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/README.md)
- Project page: registration uses "only the visual guidance of RGB images and physical constraints" and works for "garments with various topologies". — [Project page](https://eth-ait.github.io/Gaussian-Garments/)

**Stage 3: appearance model (code-verified)**
- The appearance model is `AvatarNet`, a StyleUNet (`SWGAN_unet`, borrowed from Animatable Gaussians). Input is UV-space maps: a **baked ambient-occlusion map** (baked in Blender/bpy from the garment mesh *together with the body mesh*) plus the **normal map** transformed into camera coordinates. A small conv net encodes the local view-direction map and injects it in the middle. Output per texel: an xyz offset (3) and an SH color offset. Final Gaussian color = albedo SH texture + predicted offset. The model is conditioned on geometry, AO and view, **not on SMPL pose**, so it transfers to any simulated geometry. — [avatar_net.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/avatar_net.py); [dataloader.py (bake_texture)](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/dataloader.py)
- The project page describes the inputs as "the normal map and ambient occlusions of both the inner and outer surfaces". The "predicted color offsets effectively decouple the lighting effects from the albedo Gaussian textures." — [Project page](https://eth-ait.github.io/Gaussian-Garments/)
- Training: the dataset is a shuffled list of (sequence, frame, camera) samples. Each sample needs the registered mesh for that frame and one image from one camera. Losses: L1+SSIM, an xyz-offset norm penalty, a scale penalty and an opacity penalty. Defaults are 5 epochs with a 3,000-iteration pretrain. — [s3_appearance.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/s3_appearance.py); [dataloader.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/dataloader.py)

**Stage 4: behavior fitting in ContourCraft (code-verified; partly corrects the brief's assumption)**
- Stage 4 lives in the ContourCraft repo's `GaussianGarments.ipynb`. `GauGarConverter` turns the per-frame SMPL-X into AMASS/CMU-style `.npz` pose sequences, turns the Stage-2 registrations into `.pkl` mesh sequences, imports `template_uv.obj` as a garment dict, and writes the datasplits and config. Training runs with `python train.py config=finetune/<subject>`. "The whole process should take around 24 hours depending on the hardware." A single model plus materials can also be fine-tuned for several garments at once (`prepare_multigarment`). — [ContourCraft GaussianGarments.ipynb](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/GaussianGarments.ipynb)
- **Parameters optimized:** the pretrained ContourCraft **GNN weights** (Adam lr 1e-7, restarted from the `contourcraft.pth` step-45000 checkpoint; `max_iter` 46,000) **and** a per-vertex material module `pervertex.Material`. That module holds per-vertex **Lamé μ** (log-normalized between 15,909 and 63,636; init 23,600), per-vertex **Lamé λ** (3,535 to 93,334; init 44,400), per-vertex **bending coefficient** (6.4e-8 to 1.3e-3; init 3.96e-5), per-vertex **mass** `v_mass` (from density; init 0.20022, range 0.0434 to 0.7), and **per-edge rest-length multipliers** `rest_mults` for the mesh and coarse-hierarchy edges, i.e. the **rest geometry**. **Friction is not a parameter.** "Young's modulus" is represented through the Lamé parameters. — [configs/finetune/base.yaml](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/configs/finetune/base.yaml); [material/pervertex.py](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/material/pervertex.py)
- A search-result summary of the paper agrees: fine-tuning "involv[es] optimizing the GNN's parameters, material vectors, and rest geometries". Rest-geometry optimization lets the garment "better match the original size ... and avoid simulation artifacts". — [search summary of arXiv 2409.08189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08189)
- **Losses (`criterions.finetune`):** `ft.l2` squared vertex distance to the registered mesh (weight 1e13), `ft.l2_boundary` (the same on boundary vertices, 1e13), `ft.angles` (dihedral/bending-angle match, 1e8), `ft.stretching` (Green strain relative to the target, 1e3), plus ContourCraft's collision penalty (1e14), repulsion loss and intersection-contour loss. — [base.yaml](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/configs/finetune/base.yaml); [criterions/ft/](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/tree/main/criterions/ft)
- **Procedure:** the training loop **alternates** a supervised "ft" step (a rollout over the registered sequence, driven by its SMPL-X bodies; it updates the model *and* the material optimizers) with a self-supervised "long" step. The long step uses the standard ContourCraft physics losses (stretching, bending, inertia, gravity, collision, repulsion, intersection contour) on AMASS CMU sequences and updates the model only, which regularizes against overfitting. In the ft rollout the optimizer steps **once per simulated frame**, and the previous state is detached, so gradients are effectively one step long, not backpropagated through time. — [runners/finetune.py](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/runners/finetune.py)
- **Frames needed:** the converter keeps the first sequence (`n_validation=1`) for validation and trains on the rest, so **at least 2 registered sequences** are needed for the default split. Every frame of each sequence is used. The first two frames seed the initial state (`lookup = all_verts[:, 2:]`). — [utils/gaugar.py](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/utils/gaugar.py); [datasets/finetune.py](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/datasets/finetune.py)
- Base simulators: HOOD (CVPR 2023) is a self-supervised hierarchical GNN whose material inputs are Young's modulus and Poisson ratio (as Lamé μ, λ), a bending coefficient and density, changeable at inference. — [HOOD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.07242). ContourCraft (SIGGRAPH 2024) adds an intersection-contour loss and a repulsion objective, so it recovers from penetrations in multi-layer outfits. — [ContourCraft (MPI-IS)](https://is.mpg.de/publications/10-1145-3641519-3657408)

### Inferences
- The brief's belief ("fine-tune ContourCraft's material parameters") is correct but incomplete. The fine-tune also changes the **network weights** and the **rest shape (edge-length multipliers)**, and the material is **per-vertex**, not global. Friction and damping are not fitted.
- Because Stage 4 supervision is only 3D vertex positions (registrations) plus SMPL-X, **Stage 4 is independent of images**. Any source of temporally consistent 3D garment mesh sequences with the fixed template topology, paired with body meshes, could replace Stage 2's output.
- A rough cost: 4D-Dress sequences average about 150 frames (78k frames / 520 sequences). At "a few minutes" per frame, registering one sequence takes several GPU-hours, and Stage 4 adds about 24 h.

### Gaps
- I could not read the paper PDF (arXiv blocked). Missing: paper-reported camera counts and resolution for the 4D-Dress captures, the exact number of sequences and frames per garment used for fine-tuning, quantitative results, ablations, and the paper's stated limitations.
- I could not confirm whether the paper used 4D-Dress raw camera images or images rendered from the textured scans.

## Q2. Which stages strictly need synchronized multi-view video, and what could run from a single static multi-view capture plus something else?

### Takeaway
Only one stage truly needs multi-view **video**: **Stage 2 registration** needs several synchronized views of *each* frame to constrain the 3D garment surface. Stage 4 needs its *output* (3D mesh sequences), not the images. Stage 1 uses a **single multi-view frame**, so a static multi-view capture (or an existing splat) is enough. Stage 3 needs the registered meshes plus *any* images aligned with them; one image per frame suffices if the mesh-image alignment is accurate. Relatives such as PGC (CVPR 2025) and DiffAvatar show you can skip video entirely: take the geometry and appearance from one static multi-view pose, and choose the material by default or fit it to the static drape.

### Cited Findings
- Stage 1 and appearance initialization use a **single template frame** (`-tf`) from the multi-view set. — [GG README](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/README.md); [Project page](https://eth-ait.github.io/Gaussian-Garments/)
- Stage 2 draws a random camera **of the current frame** at each iteration and fits photometric loss, so several synchronized views per timestep jointly constrain the mesh. Without them, only the physics-like energies (bending, stretching, collision, virtual edges) regularize the unseen side. — [s2_registration.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/s2_registration.py)
- Stage 3 samples (sequence, frame, camera) tuples independently; each needs only that frame's registered mesh and that camera's image and masks. — [dataloader.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/scene/dataloader.py)
- Stage 4 consumes `.pkl` registrations plus SMPL-X `.npz` only; there are no images. — [ContourCraft GaussianGarments.ipynb](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/GaussianGarments.ipynb)
- PGC (Guo et al., Stanford and Meta, **CVPR 2025 Highlight**) "only requires a multi-view capture of a single static frame". Garments are "hybrid mesh-embedded 3D Gaussian splats": Gaussians hold near-field shading and high-frequency detail, while the mesh holds far-field albedo and reflectance. At inference the mesh is physically simulated and shaded, and a **high-pass of the Gaussian render is combined with a low-pass of the shaded mesh render**. The results are relightable. — [PGC project page (repo source)](https://phys-gaussian-cloth.github.io/); [CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Guo_PGC_Physics-Based_Gaussian_Cloth_from_a_Single_Pose_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
- DiffAvatar (MIT and Meta, CVPR 2024): from a multi-view capture it reconstructs a segmented 3D mesh. It then uses differentiable cloth simulation to recover "body shape and pose, clothing pattern and clothing material parameters **from a single scan**", optimizing garment shape in rest-shape *pattern space*. — [DiffAvatar arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12194); [CVPR 2024](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2024/poster/29902)
- Dress-1-to-3 (UCLA and Utah, **ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2025**) starts from **one image**. It builds a coarse sewing pattern with a pretrained image-to-pattern model, generates orbital views with **multi-view diffusion** "which serve as ground-truth 3D information", then uses a **differentiable IPC garment simulator** to "sew and drape the pattern onto the posed human model, optimizing its shape and physical parameters". The result is a physically plausible rest shape that can be animated. The code was listed as "coming soon". — [Dress-1-to-3 project page (repo source)](https://dress-1-to-3.github.io/); [arXiv 2502.03449](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03449v2)

### Inferences
- Mapping this onto our pipeline (static multi-view splat + Sapiens2 labels + SAM-3D-Body proxy):
  - **Stage 1 replacement:** extract the garment mesh from our static splat (see Q6), then UV-unwrap it. The Stage-2 "appearance init" can be replaced by binding our existing splat Gaussians to that mesh's faces/UVs, or by re-fitting a GG-style UV Gaussian texture to our orbit photos with the mesh fixed.
  - **Stage 2** is the part that WAN 2.2 videos cannot supply faithfully. Each generated video is monocular, and the videos are mutually inconsistent, so there is no synchronized multi-view set for any timestep. Monocular per-video tracking (template-based, physics-regularized, as in SAFT) is possible in principle. But it gives *different* 3D motions per video, and the depth ambiguity is large.
  - **Stage 4** could be (a) skipped, running the pretrained ContourCraft/HOOD with hand-picked or VLM-predicted material (Image2Garment-style); (b) fit to static drape only (DiffAvatar or Dress-1-to-3 style, i.e. rest shape and material from one pose); or (c) fit to weak video evidence with a loss that does not require consistent 3D, e.g. a per-video 2D silhouette/flow loss, or a video-diffusion SDS/VDS prior (see the MPMAvatar-VDS entry in Q5).
  - **Stage 3** (the AO/normal-conditioned shadow net) can be trained on our *static* orbit images with the static mesh. The pose/geometry dependence of shading would then be learned from only one geometry and would generalize poorly. PGC's high-pass/low-pass split is the documented way to get dynamic shading from a single static pose.

### Gaps
- There is no published evidence (found) of Gaussian Garments being run on monocular or generated video.
- PGC's exact choice of simulation material (default, estimated, or tuned) is not in the snippets I could access.

## Q3. What Gaussian Garments outputs at inference: driving, multi-garment composition, runtime

### Takeaway
At inference, ContourCraft (the fine-tuned model plus materials) simulates garment vertex trajectories driven by **SMPL-X pose sequences** (e.g. AMASS CMU, any body shape). `inference.py` then loads each garment's Gaussian texture and appearance net, re-poses the Gaussians on the simulated meshes, filters out occluded inner-layer Gaussians, and renders. Multi-garment outfits are handled by concatenating garments in one trajectory file. No paper runtime numbers could be verified.

### Cited Findings
- The trajectory `.pkl` holds `pred` [N,V,3] (garment vertices per frame), `cloth_faces`, `obstacle` [N,B,3] (body vertices), `obstacle_faces` and `garment_names`. For multi-garment outfits, `pred` and `cloth_faces` are concatenations and `garment_names` lists the subjects in order, so the script loads each garment's checkpoint. — [GG README](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/README.md)
- Trajectories come from `runner.valid_rollout(sequence, material_stack, bare=True, n_steps=200)` with `create_postcvpr_one_sequence_dataloader(...)` on an AMASS CMU SMPL-X `.npz` (for example `01/01_01_stageii.npz`). `separate_arms=True` slightly alters poses to avoid hand-body penetration (a trick taken from SNUG). — [ContourCraft GaussianGarments.ipynb](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/GaussianGarments.ipynb)
- Applications shown: garment mix-and-match across subjects into multi-layer outfits, **automatic resizing to new body shapes**, and multi-layer simulation. "We check the visibility of Gaussians on inner layers and filter out occluded Gaussians to prevent penetrations." — [Project page](https://eth-ait.github.io/Gaussian-Garments/)
- `inference.py` uses `AvatarSimulationModel(..., texture_size=512)` with a `get_visible_mask(..., dot_product_t=-0.7)` filter, and plays back at 30 fps in aitviewer. — [inference.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/inference.py)
- HOOD is described as enabling "real-time prediction of realistic clothing dynamics". — [HOOD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.07242)

### Inferences
- Simulation and rendering are separate offline steps (simulate to a `.pkl`, then render). Real-time end-to-end use is not demonstrated in the code.
- The body used at inference is an SMPL-X mesh. Our SAM-3D-Body proxy would need an SMPL-X fit (or conversion) to drive ContourCraft with standard pose sequences.

### Gaps
- Paper-reported simulation and rendering speeds and GPU memory could not be verified (PDF inaccessible).

## Q4. Code availability, license, dependencies, compute, known limitations

### Takeaway
The code is public at github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments (about 172 stars; last code commit June 2025), but **the repo has no LICENSE file**. Stage 4 lives in ContourCraft (MIT). Heavy dependencies: COLMAP, CUDA 11.8 / PyTorch 2.1, the Animatable Gaussians rasterizer and StyleUNet, pytorch3d, **bpy (Blender) for AO baking**, and SMPL-X. There are two manual steps: UV unwrapping, and (for ActorsHQ) choosing a clean template frame. The known practical failure point is Stage 1 on new data, which the authors themselves replaced for ActorsHQ.

### Cited Findings
- Repo: `eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments`, created 2024-08-24, 172 stars, 11 forks, 4 open issues (GitHub API, Sept 2026). A redirect repo exists at `Ribosome-rbx/Gaussian-Garments`. — [GitHub search API result](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments)
- Repo contents: `s1_initialisation.py`, `s2_registration.py`, `s3_appearance.py`, `inference.py`, `lbs.py`, `scene/`, `utils/` and so on. **No LICENSE file** is present (checked at commit ceb66fc, 2025-06-16). ContourCraft ships an **MIT License** (© 2023 Artur Grigorev). PhysAvatar is MIT except for bundled Codim-IPC and the rasterizer. — [GG repo](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments); [ContourCraft LICENSE](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/LICENSE); [PhysAvatar LICENSE.md](https://github.com/y-zheng18/PhysAvatar/blob/main/LICENSE.md)

## Q5. Related and follow-up work: inputs, whether they learn physics/material from video, and whether they could work from monocular or generated video

### Takeaway
There are two families. **(A) Capture-based, physics from multi-view video:** Gaussian Garments, PhysAvatar, MPMAvatar. All need synchronized multi-view video plus mesh tracking, and they fit only a handful of parameters from about 25 frames. **(B) Static or generative, simulation-ready without video:** PGC, DiffAvatar, Dress-1-to-3, Image2Garment, Garment3DGen, GarmentDreamer, ClotheDreamer, SimAvatar, ChatGarment. These get geometry from one pose or image and take material from defaults, static drape fitting or VLM prediction. Methods that fit physics from **monocular** video exist for isolated cloth (SAFT, ICCV 2025) or general objects (ReconPhys, 2026), but not for full clothed humans with a sim-ready garment. One hobby repo (2026) replaces MPMAvatar's multi-view loss with a **Wan 2.2 I2V score-distillation loss**, which matches our idea.

### Cited Findings

**Same lab (ETH AIT / MPI-IS)**
- **HOOD** (CVPR 2023): GNN with hierarchical message passing, self-supervised physics loss, real-time. Handles loose garments, topology changes (buttons, zippers), and material given as input at inference. Input: garment template plus SMPL motion. **No video; material is set, not learned.** — [HOOD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.07242)
- **ContourCraft** (SIGGRAPH 2024): HOOD plus an intersection-contour loss and repulsion, for robust multi-layer outfits. **No video.** Code is MIT and includes the Gaussian Garments fine-tuning. — [ContourCraft](https://is.mpg.de/publications/10-1145-3641519-3657408); [repo](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft)
- **4D-Dress** (CVPR 2024): the data source for Gaussian Garments (see Q1). — [4D-Dress](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Wang_4D-DRESS_A_4D_Dataset_of_Real-World_Human_Clothing_With_Semantic_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)
- **Gaussian Wardrobe** (arXiv 2603.04290, 5 Mar 2026; ETH Zürich and HKUST): compositional 3D Gaussian avatars **from multi-view videos**. Bodies plus layers of "shape-agnostic neural garments" for free-form garments (skirts, dresses, open jackets) and virtual try-on across subjects. The snippet describes neural (learned) dynamics, not a physics simulator. — [Gaussian Wardrobe arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04290); [project page](https://eth-ait.github.io/GaussianWardrobe/)
- **ChatGarment** (CVPR 2025, MPI-IS): a VLM fine-tuned to output **GarmentCode** JSON sewing patterns from a single image or text. It is evaluated on Dress4D and CloSE, and the patterns can be simulated. — [ChatGarment CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Bian_ChatGarment_Garment_Estimation_Generation_and_Editing_via_Large_Language_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.html)

**Physics from multi-view video**
- **PhysAvatar** (Zheng, Zhao, Yang, ..., Wetzstein; ECCV 2024): multi-view video (ActorsHQ, 4 subjects: 2 loose dresses, 2 two-piece outfits). Pipeline: mesh-aligned 4D Gaussian mesh tracking → garment extracted from the tracked full-body mesh (**garment segmentation and boundary/attachment points defined by hand**) → **Codim-IPC** simulation (StVK membrane plus discrete-shell hinge bending) → estimation of **density, membrane stiffness and bending stiffness** by **finite-difference** gradients (not autodiff). Default script: 24 frames (from frame 460), about 100-160 iterations. Appearance comes from physically based inverse rendering. Code is MIT with exceptions. — [PhysAvatar arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04421); [repo README and garment_parameter_estimation.py](https://github.com/y-zheng18/PhysAvatar)
- **MPMAvatar** (Lee, Lee, Kim, KAIST; **NeurIPS 2025**): multi-view video (ActorsHQ a1, a2, a3, a6; 4D-Dress 00170, 00185, 00190, 00191 Inner). The garment is simulated with a **Material Point Method** solver (anisotropic constitutive model plus new body-collision handling). Rendering uses a canonical 3DGS with "quasi-shadowing" (AO maps baked in Blender). It reports zero-shot generalization to unseen interactions. Code-verified: it fits **D (density), E (Young's modulus) and H (a rest-shape vertical scale)** with finite-difference gradients. Default script: **25 training frames, 200 iterations**, with tracked meshes from the PhysAvatar pipeline as input. — [MPMAvatar NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ccdfe117c6729267c1595cdf0a587da8-Abstract-Conference.html); [repo](https://github.com/KAISTChangmin/MPMAvatar); [train_material_params.py and scripts/physics/actorshq_a1.sh](https://github.com/KAISTChangmin/MPMAvatar/blob/main/train_material_params.py)
- **MPMAvatar + Video Distillation Sampling** (unofficial hobby repo `SamipSGz/mpm-avatar-vds`, July 2026): "Replace the MSE geometric loss in `train_material_params.py` with **Score Distillation Sampling via a frozen Wan 2.2 I2V (14B)**". It adds **friction** as a fourth parameter besides D, E and H, and optimizes with SPSA (100 iterations on ActorsHQ a1, frames 460-469). It loads only the low-noise expert to save about 28 GB of VRAM, adds penetration, stretch and temporal-smoothness regularizers, and uses a SegFormer garment split. **No published results or validation found.** — [mpm-avatar-vds CHANGES.md](https://github.com/SamipSGz/mpm-avatar-vds)
- **GausSim** (ICCV 2025): a neural simulator for **elastic objects** represented as Gaussian kernels (hierarchical center-of-mass systems, mass and momentum conservation). Trained on the READY multi-view video dataset. Not garment-specific. — [GausSim ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Shao_GausSim_Foreseeing_Reality_by_Gaussian_Simulator_for_Elastic_Objects_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)

**Static capture or single image to simulation-ready garment (no dynamics learned from video)**
- **PGC** (CVPR 2025 Highlight, Stanford and Meta): single static multi-view pose → garment mesh plus mesh-embedded Gaussians plus albedo/reflectance → simulate and relight (see Q2). — [PGC](https://phys-gaussian-cloth.github.io/)
- **DiffAvatar** (CVPR 2024): from a single segmented multi-view scan, differentiable simulation recovers the pattern, body and material (see Q2). — [DiffAvatar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12194)
- **Dress-1-to-3** (SIGGRAPH / TOG 2025): single image → sewing pattern plus multi-view diffusion → differentiable IPC optimization of pattern shape and physical parameters. Adds texture generation and human-motion generation for dynamic demos (see Q2). — [Dress-1-to-3](https://dress-1-to-3.github.io/)
- **Image2Garment** (arXiv 2601.09658, Jan 2026): single image → **ChatGarment** sewing pattern → drape with a standard simulator. A fine-tuned VLM predicts fabric composition and attributes (family, structure, areal density, thickness), and a learned **material-to-physics** mapping converts them to simulator parameters. New datasets FTAG (16,026 images) and T2P. No iterative optimization. — [Image2Garment arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09658)
- **Garment3DGen** (Meta): base template mesh plus a single image (real or generated) → image-to-3D diffusion target → mesh deformation that preserves topology and quality → drapeable, simulatable garment plus texture. About 5 min on an H100. — [Garment3DGen arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.18816)
- **GarmentDreamer** (arXiv 2405.12420): text → 3DGS-guided deformation of a garment template into a "wearable, simulation-ready" mesh plus a neural texture field (VSD). — [GarmentDreamer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12420). **ClotheDreamer** (arXiv 2406.16815): text → Disentangled Clothe Gaussian Splatting (DCGS), "supports physically accurate animation". — [ClotheDreamer](https://huggingface.co/papers/2406.16815.md)
- **SimAvatar** (NVIDIA, CVPR 2025): text → layered SMPL body, diffusion-generated garment mesh and hair strands, with 3DGS attached to each layer. Physics simulators drive the garment and hair, and motion is transferred to the Gaussians. — [SimAvatar](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dair/simavatar)
- **GarVerseLOD** (arXiv 2411.03047): a single in-the-wild image → a standalone garment mesh using a 6,000-model artist dataset with levels of detail. Geometry only, no material. — [GarVerseLOD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03047)

**Avatar methods with separate garment layers (learned deformation, not a physics simulator)**
- **D3GA** (Drivable 3D Gaussian Avatars): multi-view video, garment decomposition, tetrahedral cages with correction fields. — [D3GA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.08581)
- **LayGA** (SIGGRAPH 2024): separate body and clothing Gaussian-map layers from **multi-view videos**, for animatable clothing transfer. — [LayGA](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07319v1)
- **Monocular garment-separated Gaussian avatars (2025-26):** MonoCloth (arXiv 2508.04505), SMAGA (secondary-motion-aware, autoregressive graph deformation), Cloth-HUGS (arXiv 2604.15875, "physics-inspired cloth refinement"), GGAvatar, RealityAvatar. All learn from **monocular video** with learned, not simulated, dynamics. — [MonoCloth](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04505); [SMAGA](https://openreview.net/forum?id=2A3Q2EtGTF); [Cloth-HUGS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15875)
- **Forwardrobe** (arXiv 2607.29106, 31 Jul 2026): feed-forward garment-aware Gaussian avatar from a **single image**. The garment layer is separated in canonical space, with pose-conditioned non-rigid deformation; it targets skirts and dresses. About 10 s reconstruction, 40K Gaussians. — [Forwardrobe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29106)

**Physics from monocular video (not full clothed humans)**
- **SAFT** (ICCV 2025): given a template, fabric geometry and PBR appearance from a **single monocular RGB video** via physical cloth simulation plus differentiable rendering. Two new regularizers against depth ambiguity; 2.64× lower 3D error than prior work. — [SAFT ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Stotko_SAFT_Shape_and_Appearance_of_Fabrics_from_Template_via_Differentiable_ICCV_2025_paper.html)
- **ReconPhys** (arXiv 2604.07882, 2026): feed-forward 3DGS plus physical attributes from a single monocular video, in under 1 s (general objects). — [ReconPhys](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.07882)

**Other items in the brief**
- **ReMP** (WACV 2025) is a *Reusable Motion Prior* for 3D human pose estimation and motion inbetweening from depth, LiDAR or IMU. It is **not** a garment method, but it could fill body-pose gaps. The brief may have meant a different "ReMP". — [ReMP WACV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/html/Jang_ReMP_Reusable_Motion_Prior_for_Multi-Domain_3D_Human_Pose_Estimation_WACV_2025_paper.html)
- **SNUG** (CVPR 2022): the self-supervised predecessor of HOOD. Its only confirmed appearance here is the `separate_arms` trick adopted from SNUG's code. — [ContourCraft notebook](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/ContourCraft/blob/main/GaussianGarments.ipynb)

### Inferences
- Summary on video needs:

| Method | Input | Learns material from video? | Monocular or generated video workable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian Garments | sync multi-view video (4D-Dress) | yes (GNN plus per-vertex material plus rest shape) | only via a replacement Stage 2 |
| PhysAvatar | sync multi-view (ActorsHQ) | yes (3 params, finite differences, 24 frames) | no (needs mesh tracking) |
| MPMAvatar | sync multi-view | yes (D, E, H; 25 frames) | the VDS hack tries a Wan 2.2 SDS loss; unvalidated |
| PGC | single static multi-view pose | no | not needed (static) |
| DiffAvatar | single segmented scan | material from static drape | not needed |
| Dress-1-to-3 | single image plus multi-view diffusion | shape and physical params from static generated views | yes: generated views, static only |
| Image2Garment | single image | VLM-predicted material | not needed |

- Across these papers, material is fitted from few frames (about 24-25) and few parameters (3-4). This suggests that even weak or inconsistent video evidence could constrain *global* parameters, e.g. a per-video loss averaged over several WAN videos. Per-vertex fields like Gaussian Garments' would overfit noise.
- The PGC + Dress-1-to-3 + Image2Garment combination is the closest documented match to our situation: static multi-view geometry and appearance, generative views for shape, and a material prior instead of video fitting.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed work that fits garment material from **mutually inconsistent generated (video-diffusion) monocular videos** of a clothed human. The only instance found is the unvalidated hobby repo above.
- I did not verify which image-to-sewing-pattern model and which multi-view diffusion model Dress-1-to-3 uses, or its code status after 2025.
- "PhysMesh" (Springer chapter, simulation-ready meshes from monocular video) appeared in one search result but could not be verified.

## Q6. How to extract a simulation-ready garment mesh (optionally with sewing pattern) from an existing splat plus segmentation labels

### Takeaway
The authors' own ActorsHQ Stage 1 is literally "splat → mesh": it trains a 3DGS, renders **stereo pairs**, estimates depth, masks the garment with GroundingDINO+SAM, fuses with TSDF, cleans, and ACVD-remeshes. Applied to our splat, this is gs2mesh, with the masks replaced by Sapiens2-label renders. After that you need: an open, single-layer (non-watertight) surface; removal of body-side and inner faces; a remesh to about 8k vertices; and a UV unwrap. For a sewing pattern, fit GarmentCode or ChatGarment-style patterns (Image2Garment, Dress-1-to-3) or use point-cloud-to-pattern methods (NeuralTailor, SewPCT, InverseDraping).

### Cited Findings
- ActorsHQ-for-GG Stage 1: its own COLMAP sparse model with **known extrinsics** → gs2mesh stages "Gaussian Splatting, Rendering, Masking, TSDF" → `template.obj` plus `point_cloud.ply`. `--masker_prompt` is a GroundingDINO prompt, and `--garment_type` is one of upper, lower or dress. Tuning advice for `TSDF_cleaning_threshold` is given. — [ActorsHQ-for-GG README](https://github.com/hlimach/ActorsHQ-for-Gaussian-Garments)
- **GS2Mesh** (Technion): renders stereo-aligned image pairs from a trained 3DGS at the training poses, runs a pretrained stereo model (DLNR) for depth, and fuses with TSDF plus Marching Cubes. The result is smoother and more accurate than direct extraction from Gaussians; SOTA on Tanks and Temples and DTU. — [GS2Mesh arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01810)
- Gaussian Garments' native Stage 1 garment cut: Poisson mesh → `parse_scan` using per-view garment masks → keep the largest connected component → pyacvd to 8,000 vertices. — [initialisation_utils.py](https://github.com/eth-ait/Gaussian-Garments/blob/main/utils/initialisation_utils.py)
- **GarmentGS** (ICMR 2025): dense point-cloud-guided Gaussian splatting that produces **non-watertight, single-layer garment meshes** with high geometric accuracy, in about 10 minutes. — [GarmentGS arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02126)
- Sewing-pattern recovery from 3D: **NeuralTailor** (TOG 2022, point cloud → pattern structure), **SewPCT** (2025, transformer on point clouds), **InverseDraping** (arXiv 2604.02764, 2026: point cloud → BoxMesh, a panel-structured 3D intermediate → parametric patterns with stitches via an autoregressive model). — [NeuralTailor](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3528223.3530179); [SewPCT](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-82021-2_15); [InverseDraping](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02764)
- Image-based pattern estimation: **ChatGarment** (GarmentCode JSON from an image), **DressWild** (arXiv 2602.16502, feed-forward, pose-agnostic patterns from in-the-wild images). Image2Garment and Dress-1-to-3 then drape and refine these patterns with simulation. — [ChatGarment](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Bian_ChatGarment_Garment_Estimation_Generation_and_Editing_via_Large_Language_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.html); [DressWild](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16502); [Image2Garment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09658); [Dress-1-to-3](https://dress-1-to-3.github.io/)
- Template-deformation route: Garment3DGen deforms a base garment mesh toward a 3D target while keeping it simulatable; GarmentDreamer uses 3DGS guidance for template deformation. — [Garment3DGen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.18816); [GarmentDreamer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12420)
- PhysAvatar alternative: extract the garment from a tracked full-body mesh (`extract_cloth.py`) using hand-made garment segmentation and boundary/attachment vertices, then compute LBS weights by weight inpainting for the body. — [PhysAvatar README](https://github.com/y-zheng18/PhysAvatar)

### Inferences
- A practical recipe for our data, not taken from any single paper:
  1. Render our splat from the orbit poses as stereo pairs, get depth with gs2mesh or a direct splat-to-mesh method, and render per-view garment masks by splatting only the Gaussians with Sapiens2 garment labels. This is cleaner than GroundingDINO.
  2. Fuse into a TSDF, or Poisson-reconstruct a full clothed mesh and cut it with multi-view label voting, as `parse_scan` does.
  3. Remove the inner, body-facing shell: faces inside or near the SAM-3D-Body/SMPL-X proxy, or back-faces of the thickened surface. The goal is a single-layer open garment surface with openings at the neck, arms and waist. HOOD/ContourCraft expect a thin-shell mesh.
  4. ACVD remesh to about 5-10k near-uniform triangles (GG uses 8k).
  5. UV unwrap (Blender, or automatic xatlas/Blender Smart UV if seams do not matter).
  6. Optionally fit a GarmentCode pattern via ChatGarment or InverseDraping for a true rest shape. Otherwise, treat the captured draped shape as the rest shape and let ContourCraft's rest-length multipliers or MPMAvatar's H-style rest scaling absorb the difference.
- Our splat was captured in one static pose, so the garment's "rest shape" is really a draped, gravity-loaded shape. Gaussian Garments' rest-geometry fine-tuning, DiffAvatar/Dress-1-to-3's pattern-space optimization and MPMAvatar's H parameter all exist to correct this.

### Gaps
- I found no published method that takes a **Gaussian splat plus per-Gaussian semantic labels** and outputs a sewing pattern end to end. That recipe is assembled from parts.
- I did not verify how GarmentGS handles garment-body separation, or whether its code is released.
